| 29 | INFORMATION | Gwanghuimun | Gwanghuimun, located southeast of Seoul City Wall, was called Sigumun (“Gate of coffins”) because the bodies of the deceased were transported beyond the wall via this gate.   Part of the gate was destroyed during the Japanese Colonial Period, and in the 1960’s, during a street expansion project, the remaining part was dismantled. Then, in 1975, the gate was rebuilt at the current location, some 15m south of its original position.  People were reluctant to use ""Sigumun"" because of what it symbolized, but there was a king who actually passed through this gate. It was King Injo, who escaped through here to Namhansanseong Fortress in 1636, during the invasion by the Manchus.   Sindan-dong is also a designation related to Gwanghuimun. The houses of shamans who gave comfort to the souls of the deceased were situated outside this gate, so the area was named Sindang (“Shaman House”)-ri. However, it was renamed Sindang(“New House”)-ri later. |

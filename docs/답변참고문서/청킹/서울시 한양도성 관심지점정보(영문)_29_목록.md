| 49 | INFORMATION | Sungnyemun Gate | Sungnyemun, also known as Namdaemun (South Gate), is the main gate of Seoul City Wall thas has been designated as National Treasure No. 1. Construction of the gate started in 1395 and ended in 1398, although it was rebuilt on two occasions in 1448 and 1479. In 1907, the right and left sides of the wall were demolished for the reason of causing inconvenience to the traffic, but the gate itself remained as a cultural heritage. I had been the oldest building in Seoul until February 2008, when the wooden structure on the second floor of the gate tower was burned down by fire. It was not fully restored until May 2013. In addition, an 83m-long section of wall connecting to both sides of Sungnyemun was restored at this time. It is a 2-story building with an arch-shaped hongyemun built at the center of the shaft base, and 5 front faces and 2 side faces. The roof, when seen from the front, has a trapezoidal shape and in Korean this type of is called Ujingakjibung. The name Sungnyemun is written vertically so that the two characters, Sung and Ye, are on top of each other and appear as though there is an intense fire burning. This was to prevent the fire energy of Gwanaksan Mountain from reaching inside the city wall by overpowering it with a greater fire. |
